Volume

Definition. Let $S \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a bounded solid region whose boundary has zero volume (a Jordan measurable region). The volume of $S$ is defined as the triple integral

$$ V(S) = \iiint_S \, dV = \iiint_S \, dx\,dy\,dz, $$

provided this integral exists. When $S$ is the solid of revolution generated by rotating the region under $y = f(x) \geq 0$ on $[a,b]$ about the $x$-axis, the disk method gives

$$ V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 \, dx. $$

More generally, for a solid bounded below by a region $D$ in the $xy$-plane and above by $z = g(x,y)$, volume is computed as the double integral $V = \iint_D g(x,y)\, dA$.

Worked example. Find the volume of the solid obtained by revolving $y = \sqrt{x}$, $0 \le x \le 4$, about the $x$-axis. By the disk method,

$$ V = \pi \int_0^4 (\sqrt{x})^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^4 x \, dx = \pi \left[\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4 = 8\pi. $$

Key theorem (Fubini's theorem for volume). If $S$ is expressible as an iterated region — for instance $S = \{(x,y,z) : a \le x \le b,\, h_1(x) \le y \le h_2(x),\, g_1(x,y) \le z \le g_2(x,y)\}$ — and the integrand is continuous on $S$, then the triple integral defining $V(S)$ may be evaluated as an iterated integral in any order consistent with the region's description, and the result is independent of the order chosen. This theorem justifies reducing volume computations to successive single-variable integrations, as in the disk and shell methods, which are special cases of iterated integration.

Lab cell (SymPy).

from sympy import symbols, sqrt, integrate, pi

x = symbols('x', positive=True)
f = sqrt(x)
V = pi * integrate(f**2, (x, 0, 4))
print(V)  # 8*pi
